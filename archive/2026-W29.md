## Causal Discovery & Structure Learning

### Learning Sparsest Linear Causal DAGs with Latent Confounders via Higher-Order Cumulants
**Ming Cai, Hisayuki Hara**
[arXiv:2607.05984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05984) | `cs.LG`

Addresses the hard problem of recovering the *exact* DAG in LvLiNGAM (linear non-Gaussian models with latent confounders), which is identifiable only up to an observational equivalence class. The key insight is that each equivalence class admits a unique *sparsest* DAG; the paper provides the first finite-sample algorithm for recovering that sparsest DAG without restricting the number of latent confounders, using higher-order cumulants. Simulation and real-data results show superior finite-sample performance over existing asymptotically consistent methods.

*Why relevant:* Directly addresses LvLiNGAM structure recovery with arbitrary latent confounders — core to the intersection of JCI, unknown intervention targets, and equivalence-class learning.

### Structure Learning on Clustered Data
**Ryan Thompson, Matt P. Wand, Veerabhadran Baladandayuthapani**
[arXiv:2607.08238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08238) | `cs.LG`, `stat.ME`, `stat.ML`

Extends continuous-optimization DAG structure learning (à la NOTEARS/DAGMA) to clustered/grouped data by introducing a differentiable graph-coupling mechanism that places global fixed-effect and cluster-level random-effect graphs, jointly constrained to remain acyclic. Establishes identifiability and asymptotic recovery guarantees, and provides a provably convergent first-order algorithm with efficient batched cluster updates.

*Why relevant:* Bridges mixed-effects modelling with score-based causal discovery; the cluster-level random effects naturally model heterogeneous intervention targets or environment-specific perturbations.

### Bayesian DAG Structure Learning with Simultaneous Shrinkage Covariance Estimation under Scale-Mixture Error Distributions in the Proportional High-Dimensional Regime
**Samaneh Nazari, Mohammad Arashi, Abdolnasser Sadeghkhani**
[arXiv:2607.08428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08428) | `stat.ME`

Proposes R-DACH: a unified Bayesian framework placing a horseshoe prior directly on the Cholesky factor of the DAG-Markov precision matrix, so that Cholesky sparsity encodes parent-set selection consistent with a topological ordering. A per-observation inverse-gamma scale mixture yields robustness to heavy-tailed observations (Student-t, Laplace, slash are special cases). A partially-collapsed blocked Gibbs sampler jointly explores orderings, sparsity patterns, and continuous parameters; simulations with p up to several hundreds confirm gains over PC-based and DAG-Wishart competitors under contamination.

*Why relevant:* Bayesian DAG structure learning in the proportional high-dimensional regime with explicit sparsity-inducing priors and robustness — directly relevant to graphical-model and precision-matrix estimation in causal discovery pipelines.

### Deep Gaussian Processes on Directed Acyclic Graphs
**Federico L. Perlino, Oliver Hamelijnck, Adam M. Johansen, Theodoros Damoulas**
[arXiv:2607.09645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09645) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`, `math.ST`, `stat.CO`, `stat.ME`

Places GP priors over functions at each node of a known DAG, naturally producing a Deep GP whose compositional structure mirrors the DAG topology. Theoretically studies prior-collapse under depth and the role of graph topology and intermediate observations on information preservation. The variational approximation explicitly models explaining-away at colliders. Applied to a latent-collider DAG, a protein signalling network, and a multi-fidelity heavy-ion collision emulator.

*Why relevant:* Provides a nonparametric Bayesian framework for causal mechanisms structured by a DAG, with explicit treatment of collider explaining-away — connects graphical model theory to flexible function estimation.

## Confounding, Deconfounding & Latent Variables

### Spectrally Deconfounded Gradient Boosting
**Andrea Nava, Peter Bühlmann, Fabio Sigrist**
[arXiv:2607.09371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09371) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`

Extends spectral deconfounding — which shrinks high-variance covariate directions that carry dense latent-confounder information — from linear to nonlinear settings via a spectral loss for gradient boosting. Shows deconfounding requires the *interaction* of spectral shrinkage and regularization (especially early stopping); provides a mixed-model / empirical-Bayes interpretation connecting LAVA-type shrinkage to random-effects adjustment. Further extends to general likelihoods and nonlinear confounding via Laplace approximations and kernel random effects.

*Why relevant:* Spectral deconfounding under dense latent confounders, extended to nonlinear predictors — directly relevant to estimation under unmeasured confounding in the high-dimensional regression setting.

### Causal Inference for Case Studies in Behavioral Health
**Shane Sparkes**
[arXiv:2607.06912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06912) | `stat.ME`

Presents a causal inference framework for N=1 observational settings under unmeasured confounding by defining *Ω estimands* — contrasts of functions of an outcome's *support* rather than its distribution — which are insensitive to confounding by design. Proves that under a structural causal model, observational and interventional supports coincide given only positivity, without measuring or adjusting for confounders. Illustrates with a cognitive behavioral therapy case study.

*Why relevant:* Novel approach to causal identification under full unmeasured confounding using support-based estimands rather than distributional ones — a different angle on the latent-confounding problem central to the research profile.

## Interventions & Experimental Design

### Estimating Causal Effects from Data Generated by Stochastic Algorithms
**Susan Athey, Guido Imbens, Zoe Ji**
[arXiv:2607.05792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05792) | `stat.ME`, `econ.EM`

Studies causal effect estimation when content (videos, chat responses) is assigned by a stochastic recommendation algorithm that can be user-tailored. Proposes an estimator that uses logged *unexposed items* together with the ratio of assignment probabilities to identify causal feature effects even when unobserved confounders jointly affect user preferences and item selection. Identification holds without requiring knowledge of the confounder.

*Why relevant:* New identification strategy for causal effects in stochastic-algorithm / observational settings with latent confounders; complements interventional causal discovery in domains where only logged observational data are available.

### Embracing Spillover: Spatial Effects in Experiments
**Samuel I. Watson, Penny Hancock, Jack Fraser-Govil, Kieran DeWalt et al.**
[arXiv:2607.06506](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06506) | `stat.ME`

Develops a unified framework for spatial experiments where treatment effects spill over between units. Causal estimands (direct, indirect, total, dose–response) are linear functionals of a spillover *kernel*; under cluster randomization the kernel's shape is identified but its level is aliased with the intercept. Derives an exact bias decomposition (level, shape-error, leakage) computable pre-data, and a scalar *level leverage* summarizing each estimand's exposure to the unidentified level. Existing approaches (Bernoulli randomization, interference-decay bounds, compact support) are cast as anchoring choices.

*Why relevant:* Causal estimand formulation and identification under SUTVA violations / interference — relevant to experimental design for causal discovery and treatment-effect estimation in networked or spatially structured populations.

### Optimal Designs with Robust Inference for Binary Treatment Effects
**David Azriel, Abba M. Krieger, Adam Kapelner**
[arXiv:2607.05768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.05768) | `stat.ME`

Under Neyman's nonparametric potential-outcomes model with binary outcomes, derives the exact variance of the difference-in-means estimator and characterises designs that minimize it. Shows balanced designs satisfying a covariate-balance condition are asymptotically optimal, and proves a broad class of blocking designs satisfies this under mild smoothness. Develops conservative variance estimators (CMH-based and Robins-type) with asymptotic tightness under local alternatives.

*Why relevant:* Formal experimental design theory for binary causal inference; the connection between covariate balance, variance optimality, and valid post-randomization inference is foundational for interventional study design.

## Causal Estimation, Meta-Analysis & Foundations

### Causal Perspectives on Network Meta-Analysis
**Ahmed Boughdiri, Francisco Andrade, Clément Berenfeld, Julie Josse**
[arXiv:2607.09200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09200) | `stat.ME`

Argues that causal estimands — defined relative to a target population and accounting for treatment-effect modifiers and center effects — should precede aggregation methodology in meta-analysis. In the network setting, this causal formulation leads naturally to *arm-level* aggregation and treatment effects identifiable without relying on the treatment network, departing fundamentally from the conventional contrast-based paradigm. Numerical studies show divergence from conventional approaches in clinically relevant settings.

*Why relevant:* Transportability-style reasoning (target population, effect modifiers, identifiability) applied to evidence synthesis — the framework connects causal identifiability to aggregate data estimation across heterogeneous studies.

### Decision-Analytical Models as Causal Models
**Maurice Korf, Myriam Hunink, Richard Post, Jeremy Labrecque**
[arXiv:2607.09397](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09397) | `stat.ME`

Formalises health economic decision-analytical models explicitly as causal inference problems, identifying the conditions under which model-implied potential outcomes approximate true causal estimands. Decomposes *decision-analytical model bias* into model-structural bias (wrong mechanism) and target bias (wrong causal parameter), and shows target bias can arise even in simple settings because the required causal parameters often lack observable analogues.

*Why relevant:* Formalises the causal assumptions underlying a widely used class of applied causal models; the identification and bias-decomposition analysis is directly relevant to causal inference methodology.

### Bayesian Donor Set Selection in Synthetic Controls
**Seul Lee, Johan Lim, Joungyoun Kim, Xinlei Wang**
[arXiv:2607.08142](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08142) | `stat.ME`

Proposes a Bayesian hierarchical model for the synthetic control method that simultaneously estimates donor weights and selects the active donor set. A hierarchical Gamma-Bernoulli prior on donor weights assigns posterior mass to simplex faces (exact zeros), enabling sparse donor selection. Establishes posterior donor-set consistency and demonstrates improved donor recovery over fixed-donor-set competitors when the pool contains irrelevant units.

*Why relevant:* Counterfactual estimation via synthetic controls with principled variable selection — adjacent to causal effect identification under unknown intervention targets and sparse graphical structure.

### Workload-Preserving Differentially Private Synthetic Data for Causal Inference via Maximum-Entropy Calibration
**Amir Asiaee, Kaveh Aryan**
[arXiv:2607.08122](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08122) | `cs.LG`

Proposes *causal workloads* — DP query sets designed around the orthogonal moments used by doubly robust causal estimators — so that privately released synthetic data preserves ATE, ATT, and subgroup causal estimands without additional privacy budget. Maximum-entropy calibration converts the released workload into reusable synthetic records; theory decomposes ATE error into sampling, privacy, workload-approximation, and calibration terms. Introduces Causal-AIM for adaptive workload selection and a noise-aware multiple-imputation procedure for valid confidence intervals.

*Why relevant:* Integrates differential privacy with doubly robust causal estimation; the moment-preservation perspective connects to semiparametric efficiency theory for causal estimands.

### Which Regularized Propensity-Score and Doubly Robust Methods Are Best Calibrated When Exposures or Outcomes Are Rare?
**M. Ehsan Karim, Wanqing Hu**
[arXiv:2607.07065](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07065) | `stat.AP`, `stat.CO`, `stat.ME`

A plasmode simulation study comparing ten regularized propensity-score and doubly-robust pipelines (OAL, GLiDeR, HAL combined with IPTW and TMLE) under a known null (true RD = 0) across frequent-exposure, rare-exposure, and rare-outcome scenarios. Finds that outcome-aware variable selection (OAL, GLiDeR) or doubly robust estimation (TMLE) best balances bias and calibration; rare-exposure settings expose the largest gaps, with LASSO-IPTW showing bias and inflated SE that TMLE corrects.

*Why relevant:* Practical calibration study for confounding-adjustment pipelines in realistic high-dimensional settings; informs method selection for causal effect estimation under positivity violations.
