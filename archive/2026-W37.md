## Causal Discovery & Structure Learning

### Beyond Stationarity in Time Series: Discovering Causal Structures and Latent Regimes via Markov Blankets
**Authors:** Lei Zan, Charles K. Assaad, Emilie Devijver, Eric Gaussier  
**Published:** 2026-09-04 | **Categories:** cs.LG, cs.AI  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05150

Introduces RCBNB-MB, a constraint-and-noise-based causal discovery algorithm for time series that simultaneously identifies latent *regimes* (contiguous intervals with a stable causal structure) and the within-regime causal graphs, using Markov blanket selection to keep the search tractable.

**Why relevant:** Directly combines Markov boundary selection with causal discovery under regime changes — touches non-stationary causal structure learning, a natural extension of constraint-based methods.

### Guide, Not Bind: Why Defeasible Priors Fail in Augmented Lagrangian Causal Discovery
**Authors:** Sairam Sundararaman, Sara Girdhar, Manit Narasimha Murthy, Samrudh N.  
**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** cs.LG  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03442

Diagnoses two fundamental failure modes of differentiable causal discovery (e.g. NOTEARS-style) when expert-knowledge forbidden-edge priors are encoded via Augmented Lagrangian penalties: sequential penalty ramping suppresses true edges before they can be checked, and the counterfactual correction remains incomplete even after repair.

**Why relevant:** Core differentiable structure learning; the paper's formal analysis directly concerns DAG-constrained optimisation and the interplay between data evidence and expert priors in causal graph estimation.

### Replicating TRACE: A Practitioner's Guide to Its Threshold and Particle Budget
**Authors:** Alex Chadyuk, Alicia Zhang, Roy Kucukates  
**Published:** 2026-09-01 | **Categories:** cs.LG  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01108

Independent replication of TRACE, a method that reads causal event-type graphs out of a pretrained autoregressive language model by thresholding per-position conditional mutual information. The replication confirms headline F1 numbers but shows the optimal threshold is pinned to the truth margin rather than a universal constant, and that the particle budget must scale with vocabulary size.

**Why relevant:** Causal graph extraction / conditional independence testing from learned models; uses CMI thresholding, linking to CI-test-based structure learning methodology.

## Interventions, Identifiability & Experimental Design

**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** math.ST, cs.AI  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03697

Develops a formal mathematical language for causal reasoning grounded in symmetries that leave causal mechanisms invariant, enabling identifiability analysis in settings that go beyond standard IID data (e.g. time series, spatial data, dynamical systems) without invoking a bespoke causal model for each case.

**Why relevant:** Causal identification theory beyond IID; formal symmetry-based extension connects to FCI/PAG-style reasoning under non-standard data-generating regimes.

### Resolution-Aware Experimental Design under Partial Identifiability
**Authors:** Sofianos Panagiotis Fotias  
**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** cs.LG  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03686

Introduces RAED (Resolution-Aware Experimental Design), which selects experiments to minimise the expected size of the *structural candidate set* under a false-exclusion constraint, rather than maximising information gain. The key result is an exact aliasing-separation theorem showing that structural and latent-information objectives can diverge under partial identifiability.

**Why relevant:** Optimal experimental design for causal structure learning under partial identifiability — directly relevant to active learning for causal discovery with latent variables.

### COSTA: Covariance-Optimized Design and Causal Inference under Network-Temporal Interference
**Authors:** Qianyi Chen, Bo Li, Yongli Qin, Jinyong Ma  
**Published:** 2026-09-02 | **Categories:** stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02032

Proposes COSTA, a joint Bernoulli spatiotemporal treatment-assignment design for network experiments with both spillovers and carryover. Under a linear exposure model, the Horvitz–Thompson bias is the negative expected weight of an assignment cut; a covariance-level MSE bound is then directly optimised over the assignment graph.

**Why relevant:** Experimental design under network/temporal interference — addresses identifiability of interventional effects in complex observational/experimental structures.

## Conditional Independence Testing & Methodology

### Embedded Conditional Independence Tests for Large Language Model Generated Text
**Authors:** Marco Simnacher, Georg Keilbar, Benjamin König, Christoph Lippert  
**Published:** 2026-09-01 | **Categories:** stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, math.ST  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00946

Proposes eCITs (embedded conditional independence tests) that embed high-dimensional / multimodal objects (text, embeddings) before applying a standard CI test, enabling tests of whether an LLM's output carries attribute-related information beyond its source text. Establishes theoretical guarantees and applies the method to detect political bias in German parliamentary speeches.

**Why relevant:** Core conditional independence testing methodology for high-dimensional and non-Euclidean objects — directly relevant to kernel/distance-covariance CI tests in causal discovery pipelines.

## Treatment Effects & Observational Studies

### Treatment Persistence Drives Estimator Performance in Longitudinal Causal Inference
**Authors:** Sergio Gaiotti, Sara Poletto, Enrico Longato, Erica Tavazzi  
**Published:** 2026-09-04 | **Categories:** stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04940

Simulation study using a structural causal model with time-varying confounding and binary treatment to compare longitudinal estimators (TMLE, G-formula, IPW, etc.) across nine scenarios varying functional complexity and treatment persistence. Finds that treatment persistence — the correlation between consecutive treatment decisions — is a primary driver of estimator performance gaps between baseline and longitudinal methods.

**Why relevant:** SCM-grounded evaluation of longitudinal causal estimators under time-varying confounding — of direct relevance to structure learning with time-varying interventions.

### Causal Inference for Heterogeneous Extreme Quantiles with Heavy-Tailed Outcomes
**Authors:** Xiaorui Wang, Juan-Juan Cai, Huixia Judy Wang, Jian Qing Shi  
**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03933

Proposes a framework for conditional extreme quantile treatment effects (CEQTEs) in observational studies with heavy-tailed outcomes: IPW quantile regression is used to estimate intermediate quantiles which are then extrapolated via extreme value theory. The key insight is that conditional and marginal potential outcome distributions share a common extreme value index.

**Why relevant:** Nonparametric/semiparametric causal effect estimation under confounding with propensity score-based adjustment — extends IPW methodology to the heavy-tailed regime.

### A Location-Invariant Estimator of Extremal Quantile Treatment Effects for Heavy-Tailed Distributions
**Authors:** Xin Yu, Shuwei Huang, Jicheng Liu, Jielin Tang  
**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** cs.LG, stat.AP, stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04018

Addresses a location-invariance gap in existing extremal QTE estimators: a common location shift of potential outcome distributions changes existing estimates even though the population QTE is invariant. Proposes a corrected causal extreme value index estimator and paired extrapolation procedure.

**Why relevant:** Causal treatment effect estimation with propensity scores at extreme quantile levels — methodological extension of IPW-based estimation under heavy tails.

**Authors:** Mohammad Alhyari, Haziq Jamil, Hans Montcho, Håvard Rue  
**Published:** 2026-09-01 | **Categories:** stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00670

Derives a closed-form, refit-free leave-one-cluster-out cross-validation procedure for multilevel Gaussian Bayesian SEMs via INLA. ### Portable Causal Fairness Across Synthetic Data Generator Families
**Authors:** Steven Golob, Sikha Pentyala, Martine De Cock  
**Published:** 2026-09-02 | **Categories:** cs.LG  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03180

Tests whether DECAF-style causal fairness constraints (defined as edge cuts on the generator's causal graph) transfer across nine generators from three families — marginals-based, GAN, and diffusion — including differentially private variants. Finds that the fairness constraints hold across families, confirming they arise from causal factorisation rather than GAN-specific artefacts.

**Why relevant:** Applies causal graph structure (edge removal = soft intervention) to synthetic data generation — relevant to interventional causal discovery and data-driven causal fairness.

### Spurious Quantum Correlations
**Authors:** Shashaank Khanna, Matthew F. Pusey, Roger Colbeck  
**Published:** 2026-09-03 | **Categories:** quant-ph, math.ST, physics.class-ph  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04157

Extends Bell's theorem to show that any classical causal structure (including those with superluminal influences) that recovers all quantum correlations must permit what the authors call *spurious* correlations — classically impossible correlations that arise purely from the causal structure, not from physical signals.

**Why relevant:** Uses the language of causal structures and ancestral graphs in a fundamental way; results about which correlations are achievable under which causal structures are directly relevant to graphical model identifiability theory.
