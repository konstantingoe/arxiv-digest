## Causal Discovery & Structure Learning

### CausalArena: Benchmarking Causal Discovery in the Foundation Model Era
**Authors:** Zi-Rong Li, Si-Yang Liu, Tian-Zuo Wang, Han-Jia Ye  
**Published:** 2026-09-10 | **Categories:** cs.LG  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11897

Introduces CausalArena, a unified and extensible benchmark for causal discovery under a common evaluation protocol. The suite spans synthetic SCMs (controlled breadth over graph families and functional forms), semantic operational SCMs (human-auditable environments beyond standard generators), and formula-grounded SCMs (explicit scientific mechanisms), plus public real-world datasets. Experiments covering classical (constraint-based, score-based), neural, and pretrained causal-discovery foundation models (CDFMs) reveal substantial ranking shifts across SCM regimes — a method strong on one benchmark type does not reliably transfer to others.

**Why relevant:** Directly benchmarks structure learning algorithms (classical PC/FCI, neural, foundation-model) across diverse SCM families; surfaces how pretraining–evaluation overlap confounds CDFM assessment — a core concern for practitioners doing causal discovery.

## Causal Inference & Estimation

### Privacy-Preserving Causal Meta-Mediation Analysis with Survival Outcomes
**Authors:** Marie-Félicia Beclin, Tat-Thang Vo  
**Published:** 2026-09-10 | **Categories:** stat.AP, stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11685

Proposes a federated causal mediation framework for right-censored time-to-event outcomes that estimates natural indirect effects across distributed sites without sharing individual-level data. A site-by-site identifiability strategy separates heterogeneity into outcome-related, mediator-related, and interaction variance components. Federated one-step and targeted maximum likelihood (TMLE) estimators accommodate data-adaptive nuisance estimation. Applied to the French National Health Data System to study methotrexate coprescription as a mediator of biologic treatment persistence in psoriatic patients.

**Why relevant:** Causal mediation analysis with formal identifiability analysis and TMLE estimation in a federated/privacy-constrained multicenter setting; semiparametric efficiency with data-adaptive nuisance models directly connects to nonparametric causal estimation methodology.

### Generative Marketing Mix Modeling: A Causal Inference Framework Linking GEO and GEM to Business Impact
**Authors:** Masahiro Kato, Daiki Honma, Taka Kato  
**Published:** 2026-09-10 | **Categories:** stat.ML, cs.AI, cs.LG, econ.EM, stat.ME  
**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11915

Develops Generative Marketing Mix Modeling (GMMM) to estimate causal effects of Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and Generative Engine Marketing (GEM) on business outcomes. The framework formalises treatment sequences over how often users see a brand name in AI-generated answers and derives sufficient conditions for identifying the resulting potential-outcome contrasts. Evaluated via simulations using real-world product recommendation queries in English and Japanese.

**Why relevant:** Applied causal inference with explicit identifiability analysis under a novel treatment-sequence formalism; connects observational and quasi-experimental data to causal effect estimation — methodologically adjacent to transportability and multi-environment causal identification.
