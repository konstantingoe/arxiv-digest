## Causal Discovery & Structure Learning

### High-Order Markov Blanket Discovery via a k-Order Relaxation of the Faithfulness Assumption
**Loong Kuan Lee, Ragavi Krishnamoorthy, Nico Piatkowski**
[arXiv:2607.26357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26357) | `cs.LG`, `stat.ML`

Proposes a new MB-discovery algorithm that replaces the standard faithfulness assumption with a weaker *k*-order faithfulness condition, allowing correct recovery of the Markov blanket even when higher-order dependences cancel lower-order independence signals. The relaxation is proven sufficient for Bayesian network structure learning and causal feature selection, and the algorithm includes a data-driven procedure for choosing the order *k*.

**Why relevant:** Directly addresses limitations of faithfulness-based Markov boundary selection, core to constraint-based causal discovery (PC, FCI); highly relevant to Markov boundary/neighborhood research.

### Asymptotic Emergence of Statistically Supported False Causal Interpretation under Unmeasured Confounding
**Mark Louie F. Ramos**
[arXiv:2607.27593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27593) | `stat.ME`

Provides an asymptotic characterisation showing that, when a true causal variable is unobserved but correlated proxies are available, increasing sample size causes constraint-based discovery algorithms to assign ever-higher statistical confidence to *spurious* causal structures involving the proxies. The result quantifies how standard causal discovery can systematically mislead as data grow, even under well-specified conditional independence tests.

**Why relevant:** Directly concerns causal discovery under latent confounding — a core challenge in the research profile on unmeasured confounders, FCI, and ancestral graph equivalence classes.

### Causal-TS: A Python Library for Causal Discovery in High-Dimensional and Nonstationary Time Series
**Mohammad Fesanghary**
[arXiv:2607.24673](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24673) | `cs.LG`

Introduces Causal-TS, an open-source library providing four algorithms (CDNOTS, CDNOTS+, CEDAR, GRACE) plus wrappers for GES, Granger, LASSO-VAR, and LGES, all sharing a unified conditional independence test layer with GPU acceleration. Includes a regime discovery pipeline that detects structural breaks and re-runs discovery per stationary segment, handling nonstationarity in a principled way.

**Why relevant:** Practical toolbox for constraint-based and score-based causal discovery in time series; the unified CI test layer is directly relevant to reproducible experimental design for causal structure learning.

### Causal Discovery with Inverted Self-attention for Multivariate Time Series
**Yusen Liu, Yong Wang, Yifan Yin, Tianqing Zhu, Xiufeng Liu**
[arXiv:2607.28212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28212) | `cs.LG`

Proposes a transformer-based causal discovery framework where an *inverted* self-attention mechanism operates over variable dimensions rather than time steps, allowing the model to learn nonlinear, lagged causal relationships among variables. The approach captures both contemporaneous and lagged dependencies without assuming linearity or Gaussianity.

**Why relevant:** Addresses nonparametric causal discovery in high-dimensional time series; complements constraint-based approaches with a score-based neural alternative.

### EvoCause: LLM-Guided Evolution of Causal Graphs for Root Cause Analysis
**Lei Zan, Keli Zhang, Shifeng Xie, Jiale Zheng, Zehao Xiao**
[arXiv:2607.27290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27290) | `cs.LG`

Closes the feedback loop in observational-log-based RCA by letting expert diagnoses of historical incidents guide iterative mutation and selection of a causal graph. An LLM proposes local graph edits (add/remove/reverse edges) consistent with domain knowledge; the population is then evaluated on held-out incidents, giving a data-plus-knowledge hybrid that improves over a fixed learned graph.

**Why relevant:** Applies causal graph learning to root cause analysis — directly aligned with the JCI and localized RCA framing in the research profile.

## Conditional Independence Testing & Kernel Methods

### Distance Profile Embedding for Independence and Conditional Independence Testing of Random Objects
**Wenxi Tan, Bing Li, Lingzhou Xue**
[arXiv:2607.28981](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28981) | `math.ST`

Introduces the Distance Profile Embedding (DPE), which maps random objects from arbitrary metric spaces into a Hilbert space of square-integrable functions while provably preserving full distributional information. DPE enables consistent independence and conditional independence tests for non-Euclidean data (networks, trees, distributions), with a permutation-based testing procedure and theoretical guarantees.

**Why relevant:** Directly extends the toolkit for conditional independence testing — a cornerstone of constraint-based causal discovery — to non-Euclidean random objects, complementing distance covariance and HSIC-based tests.

---

### Minimax Lower Bounds of Kernel Discrepancy Estimation: MMD, HSIC, KSD
**Jose Cribeiro-Ramallo, Florian Kalinke, Zoltán Szabó**
[arXiv:2607.24235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24235) | `math.ST`, `stat.ML`

Establishes minimax lower bounds for estimating MMD (two-sample), HSIC (independence), and KSD (goodness-of-fit) in general metric spaces and function classes beyond bounded kernels. Shows that the well-known *n*^{-1/2} rate is not always achievable and characterises the sharp dependence on kernel properties, providing a theoretical foundation for kernel-based test design.

**Why relevant:** Fundamental statistical limits for HSIC-based independence testing used in causal discovery; informs choice of kernels and sample-size requirements for CI tests.

## Interventions & Counterfactuals

### DoTime: A Synthetic Benchmark Generator for Interventional and Counterfactual Time Series
**Dennis Thumm, Billy Tim Anthony, Ying Chen**
[arXiv:2607.27263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27263) | `cs.LG`, `stat.ML`

Presents DoTime, a generator of multivariate temporal structural causal models (TSCMs) with interventions, released as a PyPI package with four frozen evaluation benchmarks. TSCMs are parameterised by graph structure, noise type, and intervention mechanism, enabling controlled comparison of estimators for interventional and counterfactual queries at arbitrary time horizons.

**Why relevant:** Provides ground-truth benchmarks for interventional causal inference in time series — directly useful for evaluating methods that target interventional distributions under unknown soft-intervention targets.

---

### Evidence-Type Competition: When Can Interventional Data Teach Language Models Causal Direction?
**Xining Xun**
[arXiv:2607.29484](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29484) | `cs.LG`

In a controlled synthetic Simpson's-paradox setting, pits observational correlation against interventional signal during LLM pre-training. Finds that increasing the fraction of interventional samples monotonically strengthens the do()-response magnitude but does *not* correct causal direction when observational and interventional signals oppose each other, suggesting that the LLM encodes both signal types independently rather than integrating them causally.

**Why relevant:** Probes identifiability from interventional vs.\ observational data — relevant to understanding when interventional data actually resolves direction ambiguity in causal discovery.

## Treatment Effects & Causal Identification

### The Confounder Trap: Treatment-Encoding Representations in Causal Inference with Text
**Marie Neubrander, Graham Tierney, Alexander Volfovsky**
[arXiv:2607.26309](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26309) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`

Shows that learning representations from full text to capture latent confounders backfires when treatment status is itself lexically signalled: richer representations encode treatment directly, making the confounder adjustment collapse. Proposes a diagnostic procedure and representation-design guidelines that disentangle confounder and treatment information.

**Why relevant:** Highlights a subtle identifiability failure when text is used as a proxy for latent confounders — directly relevant to the research profile on latent confounding and identification.

---

### Inference on Counterfactual Distributions Using Martingale Posteriors
**Gregor Steiner, Mark Steel**
[arXiv:2607.24143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24143) | `stat.ME`

Extends the martingale posterior framework to counterfactual queries by pairing flexible nonparametric density estimation with instrumental-variable identification, enabling posterior inference over entire counterfactual outcome distributions rather than just means. The approach supports quantile effects, distributional differences, and derived summaries under weak parametric assumptions.

**Why relevant:** Nonparametric Bayesian approach to full counterfactual distribution identification with instrumental variables — connects to semiparametric and nonparametric methods in the research profile.

---

### Doubly Robust Functional Representation Learning for Longitudinal Causal Inference with Irregular Histories
**Mengfei Ran, Yifeng Shen, Ruijie Guan**
[arXiv:2607.28567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28567) | `stat.ME`, `stat.ML`

Proposes DR-FRL, a cross-fitted workflow for time-varying treatment estimation when treatment histories are recorded as irregular functional fragments (unequal, informative measurement times). Leverages functional data representations for propensity and outcome models while maintaining doubly robust efficiency guarantees under irregular observation.

**Why relevant:** Addresses causal identification under irregular longitudinal confounding — extends doubly robust methods to a practically important but technically demanding observational data structure.

---

### Doubly Robust Estimators of Quantile Treatment Effects with Semiparametric Cumulative Probability Models
**Hao Wu, Chun Li, Bryan E. Shepherd**
[arXiv:2607.27633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27633) | `stat.ME`

Develops doubly robust estimators for quantile treatment effects (QTEs) by modelling the conditional CDF of potential outcomes with semiparametric cumulative probability models. The estimator inherits double robustness: it remains consistent if either the propensity or the outcome model is correctly specified, and is semiparametrically efficient when both are.

**Why relevant:** Distributional causal effects estimation with DR guarantees; semiparametric efficiency results are methodologically adjacent to the research profile's interest in efficient estimators.

---

### A Novel Approach to Instrumental Variable Estimation: TEAM-IV
**Steven Y. Alberding, Patrick J. Breheny**
[arXiv:2607.28289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28289) | `stat.ME`

Introduces TEAM-IV, which selects subsets of candidate instruments that appear jointly valid (satisfy the exclusion restriction together) and aggregates their estimates, avoiding the majority-validity requirement of sisVIVE and CIIV. The method is shown to give reliable inference even when only a minority of instruments are valid, with theoretical guarantees and simulation evidence.

**Why relevant:** Robust IV estimation under violation of exclusion restrictions; relevant to identification under partial instrument invalidity in settings with latent confounding.

### Analytical and Bootstrap Confidence Intervals of Double Machine Learning
**Haozheng Xu, Siyuan Ma, Qingyan Xiang**
[arXiv:2607.29456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29456) | `stat.ME`

Conducts a comprehensive simulation study comparing analytical (influence-function-based) and bootstrap confidence intervals for DML treatment effect estimators across machine learning nuisance models. Finds that the choice of ML algorithm for nuisance estimation substantially affects variance estimation accuracy, and provides practical guidance on calibrated inference.

**Why relevant:** Practical guidance for DML inference — directly applicable when using double machine learning for causal effect estimation with flexible nuisance models.

## Mediation Analysis & Path-Specific Effects

### On the Magnitude, Sign and Ranking of Recanting-Twin Path-Specific Effects
**Tran Trong Khoi Le, Pham Hien Trang Tu, Nhat Long Ngo, Tat-Thang Vo**
[arXiv:2607.25709](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25709) | `stat.ME`

Systematically characterises when recanting-twin path-specific effects (PSEs) share the magnitude, sign, and rank ordering of their corresponding natural PSEs, closing an open question in causal mediation theory. Provides necessary and sufficient conditions under which the identifiable recanting-twin decomposition faithfully reflects the underlying causal pathway structure.

**Why relevant:** Addresses identifiability and interpretation of path-specific effects under intermediate confounding — connects to ancestral graph structure and mediation within graphical causal models.

---

### Causally Interpretable Meta-Mediation Analysis with Missing At Random Mediator and Outcome Data
**Marie-Félicia Beclin, Apolline Courrèges-Vartanian, Geneviève Lefebvre, Tat-Thang Vo**
[arXiv:2607.25822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25822) | `stat.ME`

Proposes a potential-outcomes-based framework for meta-analysing natural indirect effects that explicitly accounts for mediator-outcome confounding (absent in standard SEM-based approaches) and extends to missing-at-random mediator and outcome data via multiply robust estimation. Clarifies the target population for pooled indirect effects.

**Why relevant:** Brings graphical/potential-outcomes identification to mediation meta-analysis; the confounding treatment is directly relevant to settings with latent common causes.

---

### Bayesian Mediation Analysis for Individualized Treatment Rules
**Emmanuel M. Rockwell, Patrick J. Smith, Michael R. Kosorok, Nikki L. B. Freeman**
[arXiv:2607.28804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28804) | `stat.ME`

Extends causal mediation decomposition to the setting of individualized treatment rules (ITRs): defines natural direct and indirect components of the *value contrast* between a candidate ITR and a reference rule using nested potential outcomes, and provides Bayesian nonparametric estimation with uncertainty quantification for each mediated pathway.

**Why relevant:** Combines heterogeneous treatment effect identification with causal mediation, using graphical causal model assumptions; adjacent to optimal experimental design for personalised interventions.

---

### Mediation Analysis with Multiple Mediators Subject to Missing Not at Random
**Yanfei Jin, Shanshan Luo, Xueli Wang, Zhi Geng**
[arXiv:2607.27669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27669) | `stat.ME`

Studies identification and estimation of joint and path-specific natural direct/indirect effects when multiple mediators are missing not at random (MNAR). Introduces graphical assumptions on the missingness mechanism within a DAG framework, derives identifying functionals under each assumption, and proposes semiparametric estimators with multiply robustness properties.

**Why relevant:** Applies DAG-based identification to mediation with MNAR missingness — the graphical assumptions and identification results are methodologically close to the research profile.

## Other Relevant

### A Mathematical Framework for Topological Causal Data Analysis
**Hugo Gobato Souto, Ioannis Diamantis**
[arXiv:2607.28161](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28161) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`

Introduces Topological Causal Data Analysis (TCDA), which separates the observation space, causal-model class, topological summary layer, and causal query. The framework generalises potential outcomes to structured outcomes (images, networks, point clouds) by applying topological summaries (persistent homology, Betti numbers) *after* specifying the causal model, preserving identifiability guarantees.

**Why relevant:** Extends structural causal models to non-standard outcome spaces; the formal separation of causal assumptions from observational summaries is conceptually aligned with graphical model methodology.
