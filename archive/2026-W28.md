## Causal Discovery & Graphical Models

### Characterizing and Identifying Separable Graphical Models
**Christopher Meek, Kayvan Sadeghi**
[arXiv:2607.01057](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01057) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`, `math.ST`

Introduces *separable graphs* and *essentially separable graphs* — a broad family of mixed graphs with directed, undirected, and bidirected edges that encode independence structures arising from feedback, latent, and selection mechanisms. Provides graphical and separational characterisations of separation equivalence, a canonical form for equivalence classes, and a polynomial-time algorithm for identifying the equivalence class of any essentially separable graph.

**Why relevant:** Core graphical model theory directly extending MAG/PAG/ADMG equivalence-class research; the framework subsumes several existing graph families and offers a unified basis for causal discovery under latent confounders and selection bias.

### Learning Gaussian Graphical Models from a Glauber Trajectory Without Mixing
**Eric Shen, Tony Wu, Mahbod Majid, Ankur Moitra et al.**
[arXiv:2606.31230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31230) | `cs.LG`, `stat.ML`

Studies structure learning of a $d$-sparse Gaussian graphical model on $n$ variables from a single correlated trajectory of Glauber dynamics, without requiring the chain to mix. Gives a polynomial-time algorithm that recovers the conditional-independence graph with sublinear-in-$n$ sample complexity under mild sparsity and edge-strength conditions.

**Why relevant:** Extends classical i.i.d. GGM structure-learning guarantees to temporally correlated data — practically important when observations cannot be treated as independent.

### Non-parametric Recovery of Causal Diffusion Mechanisms from Steady-State Observations
**Richard Schwank, Mathias Drton**
[arXiv:2606.30467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30467) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`

Assumes a sparse multivariate diffusion system with a DAG-structured additive causal mechanism has reached equilibrium, and proposes nonparametric estimators that recover the time-infinitesimal transition mechanism from cross-sectional (steady-state) observations alone. Motivated by destructive measurement settings such as single-cell gene expression.

**Why relevant:** Causal structure/mechanism recovery from purely observational cross-sectional data under a nonparametric additive noise model — connects causal discovery to continuous-time latent dynamics.

### Simulating Node Manipulations in Gaussian Graphical Models: The GGMNIRA Framework for Continuous and Ordinal Psychological Network Data
**Yiming Wu, Fei Wang, Hongyun Liu**
[arXiv:2607.00915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00915) | `stat.ME`

Extends the NodeIdentifyR Algorithm (NIRA) — which evaluates node importance via simulated intercept interventions in Ising models — to Gaussian graphical models for continuous and ordinal data, enabling intervention-based importance assessment beyond static centrality indices.

**Why relevant:** Bridges intervention simulation and Gaussian graphical models; provides a computationally tractable approach to evaluating interventional influence in a graphical model setting.

## Causal Effect Estimation & Inference

### Causal Inference for All: Marginal Estimands for Outcomes Truncated by Death
**Ruixuan Zhao, Mats Stensrud, Linbo Wang**
[arXiv:2607.00222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00222) | `stat.ME`, `math.ST`

Proposes a new class of marginal estimands for longitudinal studies where outcomes are only defined conditional on survival. Unlike survivor average causal effects (which target a latent subgroup) or while-alive summaries (which conflate mortality and non-mortality outcomes), the new estimands are population-level, causally interpretable, and identifiable from observed data.

**Why relevant:** Addresses a fundamental identifiability challenge in causal inference; the estimands and identification strategy are grounded in the potential outcomes / do-calculus framework.

---

### An Instrumental Variable Approach to Account for Informative Treatment Switching in Real-world Evidence
**Yang Liu, Andrew Ying, Zongqi Xia, Jue Hou et al.**
[arXiv:2607.00980](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00980) | `stat.ME`

Frames informative post-baseline treatment switching as unmeasured confounding and uses the baseline treatment assignment as an instrumental variable. Constructs martingale-based estimating equations that remain valid across cohorts and over time, enabling reproducible effect estimation in observational longitudinal data.

**Why relevant:** IV methodology for unmeasured confounding in a non-i.i.d. (time-varying) setting; directly relevant to causal inference with latent confounders.

---

### Residual-on-Residual Regression as a Tool for Effect Estimation in Observational Data
**Ashley I. Naimi, Qianhui Jin, Ya-Hui Yu, Sara M. Parisi et al.**
[arXiv:2606.30976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30976) | `stat.ME`, `stat.AP`

Analyzes residual-on-residual (RoR) regression — fitting separate ML models for outcome and exposure on confounders and regressing outcome residuals on exposure residuals via OLS — as a stable alternative to AIPW/TMLE under high-dimensional confounding and weak positivity. Shows RoR is consistent and can be more numerically stable than doubly robust estimators.

**Why relevant:** Connects residualization/partialling-out to the partially linear model and double machine learning; practical causal inference methodology for observational high-dimensional settings.

---

### Cross-Fitted Survey-Weighted TMLE with Design-Based Variance for Causal Machine Learning
**M. Ehsan Karim**
[arXiv:2606.30918](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30918) | `stat.ME`, `stat.AP`

Studies TMLE for the population average treatment effect under stratified multistage survey designs with flexible nuisance estimators. The central finding is that cross-fitting at the cluster level is both necessary and sufficient for valid inference; variance is obtained via Taylor-series linearization of the influence function with the PSU as the replication unit.

**Why relevant:** Cross-fitting theory in a complex sampling design context; directly relevant to survey-weighted causal machine learning and semiparametric inference.

---

### Doubly Robust Adaptive Conformal Inference for Causal Effects Under Temporal Dependence
**Andreas Koukorinis, Ricardo Silva**
[arXiv:2606.30500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30500) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`, `stat.CO`

Proposes DR-ACI, which constructs valid adaptive prediction intervals for doubly robust pseudo-outcomes under temporal dependence. Achieves marginal and conditional coverage guarantees without i.i.d. assumptions, applicable to sequential causal effect estimation.

**Why relevant:** Uncertainty quantification for causal effect estimators under distributional shift and temporal dependence; bridges conformal prediction and doubly robust causal inference.

---

### Prediction Sets for Counterfactual Decisions: Coverage, Optimality, and Conformal Prediction
**Yurui Zheng, Ying Jin**
[arXiv:2607.02206](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02206) | `stat.ML`, `cs.LG`, `math.ST`

Develops conformal prediction sets for settings where the outcome depends on the action taken (counterfactual feedback loop), so uncertainty cannot be specified independently of the decision rule. Derives finite-sample coverage guarantees and shows how optimal decision rules can be extracted from the prediction set.

**Why relevant:** Provides rigorous uncertainty quantification for counterfactual reasoning; the counterfactual feedback structure is core to causal decision making.

### Hierarchical Clustering as a Novel Solution to the Multicollinearity Problem in Observational Causal Inference
**Yufei Wu, Zhiying Gu, Alex Deng, Jacob Zhu et al.**
[arXiv:2606.30992](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30992) | `stat.ME`, `cs.LG`, `stat.AP`

Proposes using hierarchical clustering to aggregate collinear predictors into groups before regression, recovering group-level causal effects. Unlike shrinkage estimators or PCA, the approach preserves the interpretability of original causal relationships.

**Why relevant:** Practical causal inference methodology for high-dimensional observational data with correlated predictors; the constraint that estimators respect causal identifiability is the distinguishing concern.

## Transportability, Experimental Design & Foundations

### From Subgroups to Population Composition: A Transportability Approach to Effect Heterogeneity
**Michael Cheung, Candus Shi, Kara E Rudolph, Valérie Garès et al.**
[arXiv:2607.01717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01717) | `stat.ME`, `stat.AP`

Reframes heterogeneous treatment effect analysis as a transportability problem: the population ATE is expressed as a mixture of subgroup ATEs weighted by population composition, and heterogeneity is understood as the extent to which subgroup effects fail to transport. Proposes a data-adaptive method to identify and characterise which subgroups drive heterogeneity.

**Why relevant:** Direct application of transportability theory to effect heterogeneity; identifies which subgroup-level causal findings generalise to broader populations.

---

### Semiparametric Efficiency in Sequential Experiments: Characterization and Design via Average Propensity
**Jiachun Li, David Simchi-Levi**
[arXiv:2606.31190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31190) | `stat.ME`

Shows that every non-anticipating adaptive assignment policy induces an *average propensity score*, and establishes a semiparametric Cramér–Rao lower bound for estimating causal targets in sequential experiments (adaptive, balanced, or rollout-constrained designs). Augmented IPW estimators with cross-fitting achieve the bound.

**Why relevant:** Semiparametric efficiency theory for interventional/adaptive experiments; the average propensity characterisation and achievability result are directly relevant to experimental design for causal estimation.

---

### A Causal Modeling Perspective on Decision Theory
**Arvid Sjölander**
[arXiv:2606.29911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29911) | `cs.AI`, `stat.ME`

Introduces a formal framework grounded in structural causal models and potential outcomes to evaluate and compare competing decision theories. Clarifies the distinction between subjective and objective elements and provides a unified language for assessing performance in controversial cases.

**Why relevant:** Foundational work connecting SCM-based causal modeling to formal decision theory; adjacent to identifiability and the do-calculus, and to debates around causal vs. evidential decision making.
